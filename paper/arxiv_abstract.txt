TITLE (plain text for the arXiv form)

Prime polynomials in Legendre intervals over F_{2^k}[t], II: exact variances via quadratic towers and supersingular Artin-Schreier curves

ABSTRACT (standard LaTeX macros only; no custom macros)

Let $q=2^k$ and let $\mathcal{I}_f=\{f^2+s:\deg s\le d\}$ be the Legendre interval of a monic $f\in\mathbb{F}_q[t]$ of degree $d$. Chen's Open Problem 8.2 asks why the variance of the prime-power count $\Psi(f)=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{I}_f}\Lambda(P)$ deviates by a factor as large as $q^2/3$ from the Keating-Rudnick prediction. We resolve the case $d=4$ completely and unconditionally: using the quadratic tower $\mathbb{F}_q\subset\mathbb{F}_{q^2}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q^4}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q^8}$ we obtain a closed formula for $\Psi$ on every class of the Hayes group $\mathcal{G}_3$, from which $\mathrm{Var}_{\mathcal{G}^2}(\Psi)/q^5=q(q-1)$, $\mathrm{Var}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Psi)/q^5=3-3/q$, the full structure of the quadratic-twist correlations, and an elementary proof of Carlitz's cubic evaluation over $\mathbb{F}_{2^{8k}}$ follow. For $d=6$ we prove that the Legendre profile takes exactly three values governed by $[c_2=0]$ and $\mathrm{Tr}(c_4/c_2^2)$, reducing the variance to three exponential sums $A_6,X,Y$ on $U=\{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{12}}:\mathrm{Tr}\,\alpha=\mathrm{Tr}\,\alpha^3=\mathrm{Tr}\,\alpha^5=0\}$. We show that $A_6-q^9$ and $X$ are sums of Frobenius traces of supersingular Artin-Schreier curves $y^2+y=\lambda_5x^5+\lambda_3x^3+\lambda_1x$ and reduce them to $\mathbb{F}_2$-quadratic forms whose radicals are kernels of explicit linearised polynomials; the pure cubic and pure quintic parts are evaluated for all $k$, and for odd $k$ the mixed part is evaluated completely: it is governed by the factorisation type of the Gold-type quintic $v^5+v+c$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$, a $\mathbb{Z}/4$-condition on the Frobenius, and the signs are fixed through the $L$-polynomials of the curves. This proves $A_6=q^9+(q-1)q^5$ for all odd $k$. The conjectured closed forms in the remaining cases ($A_6=q^9-(q-1)q^5$ for even $k$, and $X=(-1)^{k+1}q^6+q^5$ for all $k$) are verified exactly for $q\le 128$ by an algorithm independent of the explicit formula; for $X$ we determine the structure of the corresponding order-$4$ character sums (whose Frobenius classes run through $S_6\cong Sp_4(\mathbb{F}_2)$ with exact polynomial frequencies) and reduce the closed form to an explicit conjecture. The third constant $Y=q^3m_k$ is a twisted Frobenius trace on a fixed nine-dimensional complete intersection $X=V(e_1,e_3,e_5)\subset\mathbb{A}^{12}_{\mathbb{F}_2}$ (geometrically integral, normal, with explicit two-dimensional singular locus) and is not a character sum; we discuss its weight and rank. Consequently the Legendre variance at $d=6$ obeys $\mathrm{Var}_{\mathcal{G}^2}/q^{7}\asymp q^2$, not $q^4$. A spectral-folding analysis of the pair $\mathcal{G}^2\subset\mathcal{G}$ identifies the amplification as the coherence of the Fourier coefficients of $\Psi$ along the fibres of $\widehat{\mathcal{G}}\to\widehat{\mathcal{G}^2}$, which are cosets of the $2$-torsion $\mathcal{G}[2]$; it is produced by the identity class and its translation orbit, whose size is decided by Lucas' theorem. Exact computations up to $d=10$ show that the amplification is not universal: at $d=8$ the Legendre variance is only about $2.3$ times the full-group variance for $q\le 8$.
